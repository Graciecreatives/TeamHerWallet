The problem | Two sides of the same market

Knowledge has value. Selling and funding it are still hard.

A woman who can teach a language, craft, or practical skill should not need a full digital commerce operation to earn from it. A family should not need to manually approve every small learning purchase.

Creator friction

Expertise does not automatically become income

  • Packaging a course means writing lessons, pricing, a sales page, and a checkout.
  • Payment follow-up and access delivery add work after the sale.
  • Conventional platform and payout models may reduce control or exclude some creators.
Learner friction

Delegating spending needs hard boundaries

  • Families and sponsors want relevant learning, not endless searching.
  • A general-purpose AI with an unrestricted payment credential can overbuy or buy the wrong thing.
  • Approval of every low-cost class defeats the benefit of an autonomous agent.
Opportunity: connect creator supply to learner demand with agent-native payments and owner-defined, independently enforced limits.
Sources: README.md, “The problem”; shared/landing.ts. These are product hypotheses, not measured market statistics.02 / 10

The solution | A complete learning marketplace

Two AI assistants, one direct payment rail.

01

Create

A woman describes what she teaches. The creator assistant drafts lessons, price and presentation; she edits and approves publication. Listings can be a class, a private PDF guide, or a bundle.

02

Discover

A learner or sponsor states a goal and funds a learning purse. The learning agent evaluates published classes, explains recommendations and asks for approval only above the owner's threshold.

03

Pay & learn

The contract releases a single class price to the agent wallet. The agent completes an x402 payment in Testnet USDC to the creator's wallet and the paid classroom or document unlocks.

No matching class? The agent posts a privacy-limited request, suggests free resources and can purchase a matching creator response under the same rules.
Sources: README.md, “The solution” and “Private paid PDF guides”; server/utils/learning-agent.ts.03 / 10

Creator journey | Supply-side empowerment

From “I teach this” to a paid, owned storefront.

1.

Describe the expertise: class topic, audience, format, price and schedule in natural language.

2.

Co-create the offer: AI drafts and revises lessons, payment options, title and design; creator stays in control of publication.

3.

Publish and share: the creator's authenticated account supplies the payout address; the buyer sees a class page with a protected access URL.

4.

Deliver and earn: successful x402 settlement records enrolment and opens lessons or a private PDF. Payment goes to the creator's Stellar wallet, not a LearnPurse custody account.

What creators can sell today

  • Paid classes with lessons and classroom access.
  • Private PDF guides and class + PDF bundles.
  • Single payment, repeat payment or installments for supported classes.
  • Courses created in response to anonymous learner demand.

Example: “I teach Yoruba to kids abroad” is an illustrative creator prompt, not evidence of sales.

Learner journey | Agentic demand

The agent buys the right class, not unlimited authority.

Illustrative demo: Ada wants to speak Yoruba with her grandma. Her sponsor funds a 6 USDC purse, caps each class at 5 USDC and requires approval above 3 USDC.

01

Set the goal

Learner, subject and budget rules are stored; a dedicated agent wallet and Soroban purse are created on Testnet.

02

Recommend

The agent ranks matching public classes and records why they fit; when none fit, it requests one and offers free resources.

03

Act within rules

It can buy a 2 USDC class after contract checks. A 4 USDC class pauses for the owner's approval and co-signature.

04

Show the outcome

Paid classes appear in the library; events and transaction receipts link to the Stellar Testnet explorer.

Agent autonomy is useful because the owner does not have to approve every small purchase. It is credible because the spending limits are enforced outside the model.

x402 | The core payment protocol

HTTP 402 turns a protected lesson into an agent-payable resource.

01 / Request

Agent calls GET /api/courses/:id/access for a chosen paid class.

02 / Quote

Server returns 402 with PAYMENT-REQUIRED: exact USDC amount, stellar:testnet, and payTo = creator wallet.

03 / Validate

Agent checks offer against listed price, recipient and network, with a library-level spend cap; it signs the exact payment.

04 / Settle

Agent retries with PAYMENT-SIGNATURE. Resource server asks the x402 facilitator to verify and settle the USDC transfer on Stellar.

05 / Unlock

Server responds with paid access and PAYMENT-RESPONSE containing settlement evidence; enrolment is recorded.

Why x402 matters: one payment interface serves autonomous agents and human buyers using Freighter, while the creator receives the listed price directly on Stellar Testnet. The purse release is a separate, prior on-chain transaction.
Sources: README.md, “x402 payment flow”; server/utils/x402.ts; app/utils/x402-pay.ts; server/utils/learning-agent.ts.06 / 10

Trust model | Rules before reasoning

Three independent controls bound what an agent can spend.

On-chain

1. Soroban learning purse

Holds the budget and checks per-class cap, remaining total, one payment per class, pause state and owner co-signature above the ask-first threshold. Owner can change limits and withdraw.

At checkout

2. x402 offer validation

The client verifies recipient, exact listed price and Testnet network before signing; the x402 client also applies a spend cap tied to the per-class limit.

At rest

3. Minimal agent balance

The agent wallet does not hold the whole budget between purchases. The purse releases only a single approved class price when a payment is ready.

The model selects; the contract authorizes. The demo still keeps encrypted agent and purse-owner keys server-side. Production should move owner approvals and withdrawals into the owner's own wallet.

System architecture | Where each responsibility lives

AI decides. Contracts constrain. x402 settles. Creators receive.

Experience and application

  • Nuxt studio: authenticated creators draft and publish classes, documents and bundles.
  • Learning console: owner sets goal, budget, threshold; agent recommends and initiates purchases.
  • Nuxt resource server: protects /access, verifies x402 settlement and issues entitlement.
  • Cloudflare Workers + D1/R2: accounts, listings, events, enrolments, covers and private PDF bytes.

Money and proof on Stellar Testnet

  • Soroban purse: budget and release rules independent of the model.
  • USDC: purse transfers one class price to agent wallet; x402 payment then sends it to creator.
  • x402 facilitator: verifies and settles the signed payment with sponsored transaction fees.
  • Explorer receipts: release and payment transaction references are available in agent history.
A direct buyer skips the learning agent and purse: Freighter signs the same x402 offer; paid access follows the same protected-resource path.

Evidence | What the prototype actually demonstrates

Working Testnet flows, not projected traction.

Implemented and exercised

  • Real Testnet purse tests: valid release, duplicate class, limit, budget and owner-approval cases.
  • Agent e2e: buy a fitting class; hold an above-threshold purchase for approval; avoid buying twice.
  • Request loop: no match, publish a requested class, then agent buys it within rules.
  • Student x402 flow: payment settles to creator wallet and enrolment is issued.
  • Authenticated creator publishing and private PDF purchase/download have dedicated checks.

What this does not prove yet

  • All value is Testnet USDC, not real revenue or customer adoption.
  • The public facilitator is a single external settlement dependency.
  • Demo owner keys are server-managed; the agent console uses a private owner link.
  • A final seat can fill after settlement, so recovery needs production-grade handling.
  • The payment-brain server currently runs on Node, not at the Cloudflare edge.

Why EmpowerHer | The next milestone

Turn women's expertise into an agent-accessible economy.

LearnPurse links two outcomes: women can publish and receive payment directly; families can delegate learning purchases without delegating unlimited financial control. Stellar and x402 make that link programmable and auditable.

Now: prototype

Creator assistant, learning agent, Soroban purse, x402 Testnet payments, paid classrooms, private guides and request-to-creator loop.

Next: ownership

Move purse ownership to the sponsor's wallet; keep only capped agent signing server-side. Add authenticated owner access and better payment/seat reconciliation.

Then: validation

Pilot with women creators and learner sponsors; measure time to publish, request-to-class conversion, successful purchases and creator payout reliability before mainnet consideration.
